01The opportunity

You already know what a sale is worth.

Ryan, what stood out in our conversation was not only that you want to sell more copies. It was that you understand the economics. You know what you earn on each sale, you have an audience on Facebook and LinkedIn, and you sold about 212 copies in three and a half weeks largely through your own network, without a serious promotional push.

That tells us people who know you will buy. The open question is whether people who don't know you, but need what the book teaches, will too.

I don't think the answer is simply more Facebook ads. I would rather build several routes to readers, test which ones produce sales, and put more effort into the ones that prove themselves.

You are willing to reward results rather than pay someone indefinitely without knowing if their work sells books. I am comfortable building our arrangement on that.

02Who buys it

Four kinds of buyer, four different approaches.

  1. i.
    Individual readersNew managers, supervisors, team leaders and department heads learning on the job who would use a practical framework.
  2. ii.
    People who influence what others readBook reviewers and creators on Instagram and Facebook who feature a book of the week or month, especially in leadership and workplace culture.
  3. iii.
    OrganisationsChambers of commerce, municipalities, business associations and training teams. One order of 50 or 100 copies can outweigh dozens of single sales, so it gets its own approach.
  4. iv.
    Speaking audiencesI won't promise the book will bring invitations. I will build the material so it supports them when they come.
03Phase one

Getting the book in front of people who don't know you yet.

Your 5,000 Facebook followers and 3,000 LinkedIn connections are a good foundation, but the campaign shouldn't depend on them. I would work in four parts.

A. Search and discoverability

Research the terms people use around first-time managers and leadership, tighten the wording of the book and author pages, and make sure every promotional link leads to the right place to buy. This is slow, foundational work. A few edits will not put the book at the top of Google overnight, and I won't say they will.

B. Book influencers and reviewers

I would contact at least ten relevant creators, after reading what they actually feature, not with one copied message. Some will decline, some won't answer. I will report real responses, and an unanswered message is not a result. If anyone wants payment for a feature, it comes to you for approval first, and I won't buy reviews. Each reviewer's independence stays intact.

C. Content worth stopping for

A cover image with an Amazon link isn't enough. I would make graphics from the five frameworks and fifteen chapters, the questions new managers keep asking, and the mistakes people make when they first lead a team, each adapted to the platform it appears on.

D. A book trailer, $50

One short trailer reusable across Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn and YouTube. You were worried about it looking obviously AI-made, and I share that worry. The writing, pacing and typography should suit a serious management book. You will see the direction before I finalise it, and I'll send two relevant examples so you can judge the work.

04Knowing what worked

Ten messages are activity. A purchase is a sale.

An influencer may reach 20,000 followers, and that number alone doesn't tell us if anyone bought. So I would set up tracking before spending more.

  1. 1
    A landing pageClean, mobile-friendly, and a single place to send every channel's traffic.
  2. 2
    Trackable linksTagged links and Amazon Attribution where the retailer supports it, so channels can be compared by sales, not likes.
  3. 3
    A small retargeting testOn Meta, only once there is enough traffic and permission to track, and kept separate so we can see its cost.
  4. 4
    Plain reportingWork done, responses, traffic, attributable purchases and what I recommend next.
Activity10 outreach messages
Placement1 confirmed feature
Sale1 verified purchase

Institutional sales

For a company training 50 new managers, buying is a different decision from one person buying a Kindle copy. I would prepare a separate pitch on how the book works in onboarding, workshops and development programmes, aimed at specific decision-makers, not hundreds of inboxes. Your contact in Egypt is a reason to look abroad, but I'd find the right audience and route to purchase before spending anything there.

06Payment

You suggested $2,500 for 1,000 copies. Here is a version of that.

Option A. After the setup fees, I take 30% of your net author earnings from incremental sales that can reasonably be attributed to the campaign. At your stated $7.50 per copy:

Illustrations, not forecasts. If your earnings differ by format, retailer, territory or bulk terms, the real figures apply.

What counts as "incremental"?

We agree this before anything starts. We set a baseline from your sales history, then credit what campaign links, retailer attribution, referrals and identifiable bulk orders show. I won't claim every sale during the campaign. Your own posts and word of mouth sell books too. Returns, cancellations and costs are handled the same way every time, and we review the numbers together.

Option B is a fixed monthly fee for a defined scope. Costs are predictable, but you pay whether or not sales follow. Given what you told me, I think Option A is the better start.

07First thirty days

Build it, test it, then decide.

No campaign can guarantee a sales figure, and I won't pretend this one can.

Week 1

Prepare

Review existing book pages, find search terms, set the sales baseline, build the landing page and first assets.

Week 2

Reach out

Start influencer outreach, publish the first content, and check the links and tracking work.

Week 3

Read the signals

See who replied, follow up with promising contacts and begin institutional prospecting.

Week 4

Decide

Keep what is working, drop what isn't, and run a small retargeting test if the traffic supports it.

08Measures

Not follower counts.

I would judge the work by additional sales and attributable earnings, relevant features and real reader engagement, traffic to purchase pages, the cost of any paid sale, qualified conversations with organisations, and bulk or speaking enquiries. A reviewer may feature the book weeks after I contact them, and a company may take time to approve an order. Those get recorded and followed up, not reported as guaranteed revenue.

What I need from you

Your book links and promotional materials, your sales history for the baseline, any advertising or attribution data you're happy to share, whether you can supply review copies, your bulk-purchase terms, and any publisher restrictions on ads, retailer links or promotion.

Start with a clear plan and let the evidence decide the next step.

Before any campaign activity, we agree the tracking method, the definition of an incremental sale and the payment terms. You stay in control of ad spend and paid placements.
